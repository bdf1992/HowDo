# Pre-M0.0 sizing note

Not a power analysis. M0.0 supplies the real variance; this is what the
committed analysis does on *synthetic* data, run before any trial exists so
that the shape of the sample-size problem is known while it can still change
the plan.

Generated by `analysis/pilot0001.py` on independent Bernoulli trials with a
homogeneous per-task base rate of 0.35. Reproduce with `tests/test_analysis.py`
and the sweep in this file's history.

## Minimum detectable effect by configuration

Half-width of the 95% bootstrap interval on the primary endpoint, under a true
null. The power gate compares this against δ\*.

| tasks | trials/arm | total trials | ≈ MDE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 8 | 320 | 0.101 |
| 20 | 16 | 640 | 0.073 |
| 20 | 24 | 960 | 0.064 |
| 40 | 8 | 640 | 0.074 |
| 40 | 16 | 1280 | 0.052 |
| 40 | 24 | 1920 | 0.044 |
| 60 | 8 | 960 | 0.060 |
| 60 | 16 | 1920 | 0.044 |
| 60 | 24 | 2880 | 0.035 |

**Tasks buy more than trials do.** Doubling tasks at fixed trials-per-arm
narrows the interval more than doubling trials at fixed tasks, because the task
is the unit of analysis and the bootstrap resamples tasks. Twenty tasks with 24
trials each (960 trials) gives a worse MDE than 40 tasks with 8 (640 trials).
This matters because M0.0's screening decides the task count and the temptation
under time pressure is to run more trials on fewer tasks.

## What the decision rule costs

GO requires the interval's **lower bound** to reach δ\*, which is a demanding
criterion and deliberately so — it says the effect is confidently at least worth
acting on, not merely nonzero. The price is sample size. At δ\* = 0.10 and a
*true* effect of 0.15, the observed GO rate over 30 synthetic runs was:

| tasks | trials/arm | GO on true effect 0.15 | GO on true effect 0.25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 8 | 27% | 87% |
| 40 | 16 | 40% | 100% |
| 60 | 24 | 80% | 100% |

A true effect just above δ\* mostly returns INCONCLUSIVE at pilot scale. That is
the rule working as designed — the run genuinely cannot distinguish "worth
acting on" from "not" — but it means an INCONCLUSIVE is the *expected* outcome
for a modest real effect, and the response is more tasks, not a lower δ\*.

## Why these numbers are optimistic

Every figure here is a floor on what the real study needs.

- Trials are independent Bernoulli draws with the same rate within a task. Real
  trials on one task share a fixture, a prompt, and a failure mode; correlated
  failures inflate variance.
- Per-task base rates are identical. Real tasks range from near-impossible to
  near-trivial for a 12B, and the screening rule only removes the exact floor
  and ceiling.
- No infrastructure exclusions are simulated. Real exclusions shrink the sample
  after it is planned.
- The judge is absent, correctly — confirmatory tasks are deterministic — but so
  is any verifier flakiness.

**Do not use this table as the power gate.** It is a sighting shot. The gate
uses M0.0's measured variance, as the preregistration requires.
